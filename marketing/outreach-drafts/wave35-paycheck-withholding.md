# Wave 35 outreach drafts — paycheck withholding & commission-sales niche

The hook: every "why is my bonus taxed so high?" thread gets the same wrong answer —
"bonuses are taxed higher." They aren't; they're WITHHELD higher, because payroll
annualizes: it treats this one check as if you earn it every check. A $21,000
commission check to a single filer is treated as a $546,000/year salary, pushed
through the 35% bracket, and $5,932 vanishes to federal withholding alone (35.9%
with FICA) — even if the person's real salary is $52k. The difference comes back
only as a refund next April. This calculator was verified to the penny against a

official 2026 brackets (Rev. Proc. 2025-32). That story — the phantom $546k salary —

## 5. Reddit r/sales, r/personalfinance, r/tax — community protocol, NO pitch

"Commission check got destroyed" and "bonus taxed at 40%??" threads are weekly.
Strict norms:
- Lead with the correction: bonuses and commissions are not taxed higher — they
  are WITHHELD higher. Withholding is a deposit, not the tax. The annualization
  method (this check × pay periods → brackets → ÷ periods) is the mechanism; name it.
- Give the concrete example unprompted: single filer, $21k biweekly check →
  payroll annualizes to $546k → ~$5,900 federal withholding; their normal $3k
  check withholds ~$320. The gap settles at filing time as a refund.
- Supplemental-wage nuance: if the employer pays the bonus as a separate
  supplemental payment, the flat 22% method (37% over $1M) may apply instead —
  always ask "was it a separate check or merged with regular wages?" before
  estimating.
- W-4 advice: lumpy earners can add a flat dollar amount on line 4(c) calibrated
  to the NORMAL check, or adjust after the big check lands. Never advise exempt.
- FICA is flat (6.2% SS up to the wage base, 1.45% Medicare) — it doesn't explain
  the big-check shock; don't let threads conflate it with bracket effects.
- 10+ substantive answers before any link; link only when asked "what did you
  calculate this with."

## 5. Reddit r/freelance, r/smallbusiness, r/tax — community protocol, NO pitch

Solo 401(k)/SEP threads spike every quarter-end and every March–April. Strict norms:
- "25% of profit" is wrong for sole proprietors: it's 20% of net earnings from
  self-employment (profit − half the SE tax). The 25% figure is the S-corp
  W-2 rate and the rate that produces 20% after the circular adjustment — the
  sources of endless confusion. Run the $100k example: $18,587 employer side,
  not $25,000.
- Solo 401(k) must be ESTABLISHED by December 31 (deferral election too, for
  sole props); SEP can be opened AND funded by the filing deadline including
  extensions. That's the entire reason SEPs still exist for solos.
- Employee deferral limit ($24,500 for 2026) is per PERSON across all plans —
  day-job 401(k) + solo 401(k) share one bucket. Employer sides are separate.
- SEP + employees = same percentage for everyone eligible (21+, 3-of-5 years,
  $750+ comp). Solo 401(k) is only available with no non-spouse employees.
- Backdoor Roth: SEP and SIMPLE balances count in the pro-rata rule. Rolling a
  SEP into a solo 401(k) before Dec 31 cleans the slate.
- 10+ substantive answers before any link; link only when asked "what did you
  calculate this with."

## 5. Reddit r/personalfinance, r/retirement, r/financialindependence — community protocol, NO pitch

"Should I take the annuity?" and "lump sum vs pension?" threads are weekly. Strict
norms:
- Never answer "annuities are bad/good" — ask which TYPE first: SPIA/DIA (often
  reasonable longevity insurance) vs deferred variable/indexed (usually
  fee-stacked). The answer diverges completely.
- Teach the implied-rate test: quote ÷ premium over the period, compared to safe
  yields. $650/mo for 20y on $100k = 4.82% implied. Below Treasuries → keep the
  lump; above, and it might be fair longevity insurance.
- Mortality credits: a life annuity can legitimately beat amortization math because
  early deaths fund survivors. That's the product's one real trick — price it as
  insurance, not investment.
- Pension threads: before any breakeven math, ask about the plan's PBGC status,
  COLA (a non-COLA pension loses ~half its real value in ~23 years at 3% inflation),
  and survivor options — the single-life quote is always the flattering one.
- Deferred-annuity red flags to name: surrender schedules 7+ years, caps/participation
  rates on "indexed" products, and commissions baked into the implied rate.
- 10+ substantive answers before any link; link only when asked "what did you
  calculate this with."

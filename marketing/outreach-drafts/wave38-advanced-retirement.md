## 5. Reddit r/financialindependence, r/personalfinance, r/whitecoatinvestor — community protocol, NO pitch

Backdoor/mega/SS threads are daily. Strict norms:
- Backdoor Roth: FIRST question is always "any pre-tax IRA money anywhere?" —
  SEP and SIMPLE count; spouse's IRAs don't; 401(k)/403(b)/TSP don't. Basis lives
  on Form 8606 — no 8606 history, no basis, and the $50 penalty is the small part.
- Mega backdoor: two plan features required (after-tax contributions + in-plan
  conversion or in-service rollover); "we have Roth 401(k)" is not one of them.
  Catch-up contributions can't be after-tax. Convert promptly — earnings in the
  after-tax account are taxable.
- Social Security: bend points lock in the year you turn 62 ($1,286/$7,749 for
  2026 eligibility); later COLAs adjust the benefit, not the formula. Missing
  years are zeros in the 35-year average — the most common AIME surprise.
  The earnings test withholds but doesn't destroy: withheld months are credited
  back at FRA.
- 10+ substantive answers before any link; link only when asked "what did you
  calculate this with."

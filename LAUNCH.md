# CalcStack — Launch Package

Ready-to-fire copy for launch day. Timing: after the custom domain is live and
Search Console shows the first indexed pages. Order matters: communities first,
Product Hunt once we have some social proof.

## The one-liner

**Budgeting apps want your bank login. CalcStack wants a paste.**

## Product Hunt

**Tagline:** Free financial calculators that run 100% in your browser — no signup, no tracking, no ad walls.

**First comment (maker):**

> Hey PH! I built CalcStack because every calculator site I used was an ad farm
> from 2006: popups, cookie walls, "enter your email to see results."
>
> So I built the opposite:
>
> - 500+ free calculators and data tools (mortgage, paycheck math for all 50 states
>   verified against official DOR schedules, freelance rates, sales tax, sports science,
>   construction bid sheets, physician wRVU comp…)
> - Live market quotes (Tradier) plus a portfolio tracker and a DCA-vs-lump-sum
>   simulator that runs on real historical closes
> - A mascot, Calcy, who trades by a published formula you can rewrite — he runs
>   a $100k paper portfolio AND a real brokerage account, every fill public on
>   the site within 5 minutes — and talks
> - Everything computes instantly as you type, entirely in your browser
> - Bill Analyzer: paste your bills as plain text, get totals, categories, and
>   flags on where you're overpaying — parsed locally, never uploaded
> - Every calculator is embeddable free (iframe snippet on each page)
> - No accounts. No tracking pixels. No "premium unlock."
>
> Monetization is honest: unobtrusive affiliate links and (eventually) one
> display ad slot per page, clearly labeled.
>
> Would love your feedback on which calculators to add next.

## Reddit (spread over 2–3 weeks, never same day)

Rules: real answer first, link second (or not at all), disclose it's our site,
one post per subreddit per week max, reply to every comment.

**r/personalfinance — comment strategy (not a post):**
Watch for "how much should I charge freelance" / "mortgage payment on $X"
questions. Answer with the worked math in the comment, then: "I built a free
calculator that does exactly this if you want to play with the numbers:
[link]. Runs locally, no signup."

**r/freelance — post:**
Title: "I kept undercharging, so I built a rate calculator that starts from the
salary you want instead of vibes — free, no signup"
Body: the naive-division mistake, the billable-hours reality, what the
calculator computes, link. Include 2–3 example scenarios with numbers.

**r/Frugal / r/povertyfinance — Bill Analyzer post:**
Title: "Paste your bills as plain text, get told where you're overpaying — no
app, no bank login, no account"
Body: the story (budgeting apps want your bank; this parses locally in the
browser), a worked example with sample output, honest limits (benchmarks are
rough US averages), link.

**r/SideProject — post:**
Title: "My calculator site's mascot trades real money in public — and you can rewrite the formula he trades by"
Body: build story, the transparency stack (published policy → $100k paper book →
real brokerage account live on the site → visitor-editable formula), architecture
honesty (static site, client-side JS, key-gated serverless trade pipe with
preview-before-execute). This sub rewards transparency about revenue model: state
it plainly (labeled affiliate links + one clearly-labeled ad slot).

## Hacker News (Show HN)

Title: "Show HN: CalcStack – 500+ calculators that run entirely in your browser"

Best window: weekday 8–10am ET. HN rewards technical honesty — lead with the
architecture (static site, zero backend, client-side parsing), not the money.
If asked about monetization: "clearly labeled affiliate links; no data to sell
because we never see any."
